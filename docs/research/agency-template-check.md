# Agency phishing-report template check

Checked 2026-09-21. This review looked for copyable report text, prescribed sections, and incident-report examples from FBI/IC3, CIA, NSA, and CISA. It did not submit forms or access private correspondence. Findings concern the sources below, not every agency publication.

The checked current public materials provide incident-report fields and evidence suggestions. None supplied a ready-to-send ordinary phishing abuse email with a subject, salutation, factual body, and requested provider action.

## What the sources actually provide

| Source | Verified structure | Appropriate interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [IC3 complaint form](https://complaint.ic3.gov/) | Sections for the filer, complainant, transactions, reported subjects, incident description, other information, privacy, and signature. The incident narrative allows 3,500 characters; technical information allows 5,000. Separate fields cover witnesses and previous agency reports. | A criminal-complaint intake form. It asks the reporter to describe events in their own words, without prescribing paragraph headings inside the narrative. Technical details may include email headers. It says to retain original evidence and that providing it in the form is unnecessary. The signature attests to accuracy. These fields are not an official email template. |
| [CISA voluntary cyber incident reporting](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cyber-threats-and-advisories/information-sharing/cyber-incident-reporting-critical-infrastructure-act-2022-circia/voluntary-cyber-incident-reporting) | Suggested information to gather includes the affected entity and contact; incident description covering discovery, exploited vulnerabilities, adversary methods, and impacts; technical indicators and artifacts; and mitigation with its effectiveness. Examples include domains, URLs, email addresses, and phishing messages. | Useful content guidance for an organizational incident report. It encourages reporting known information and updating later. It does not prescribe an ordinary phishing email's subject or prose. |
| [Joint CISA/NSA/FBI/MS-ISAC phishing guidance, October 2023, p. 12](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2023/231018.pdf) | Reporting section directs readers to mail-provider reporting features, CISA, IC3/FBI, and MS-ISAC as applicable. | This directly relevant phishing publication provides reporting instructions but no copyable abuse email or required narrative headings. |
| [CIA Report Information](https://www.cia.gov/report-information/) | Suggestions concern the source's identity, how they obtained information, and safe contact details. | Foreign-intelligence tip guidance. No ordinary phishing incident or provider-abuse template was found on this page. Its identity suggestions should not be imported into a routine provider report. |
| [NSA Cyber 9-Line article, June 2020](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/News-Highlights/Article/Article/2213035/cyber-9-line-improves-cybersecurity-and-enables-election-integrity/) | The article identifies a question template that participating National Guard units use to communicate incidents to USCYBERCOM. | A real named template, but for a specific military coordination workflow. The retrieved article text does not enumerate its questions. This review did not verify the linked graphic's contents, so it supplies no copied fields. |

## Applicability to provider reports

CISA's organizational incident checklist can inform evidence selection, but a provider report also needs the connection between the recipient's service and the observed abuse. The canonical [reporting guide](../../provider-abuse-reporting.md) remains the drafting source. These agency materials do not supply a replacement provider letter.

## Historical and specialized material

The [FBI's February 2002 archive announcement](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/news/pressrel/press-releases/cio-magazine-fbi-and-secret-service-announce) confirms that FBI and USSS sanctioned CIO magazine's cyberthreat response and reporting guidelines, including a report form for businesses. The announcement itself does not contain the form, and this bounded review did not recover or validate it as current guidance. Therefore, an unqualified claim that the FBI has never supported a reporting template would be wrong.

CISA also publishes a [workplace insider-threat reporting form](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/insider-threat-reporting-template_workplace-reporting-form_07-16-2024_508.pdf). It asks about an incident, location, timing, concern type, and associated people. Its purpose is documenting suspicious workplace behavior, not routine external phishing abuse. It should not be relabeled as a phishing-report template.

## Retrieval limits

CISA's voluntary-reporting page failed through the web extractor but its official HTML was retrieved directly and read. The IC3 form, CIA page, joint guide, NSA article, and FBI archive were read directly. The insider-threat form was checked through the official PDF's indexed text. No authenticated agency portal, legal reporting obligation, or machine-readable reporting standard was evaluated here. This note identifies material that can inform a report; it does not authorize sending one.
