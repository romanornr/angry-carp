# Registrar escalation requirements

Research date: 2026-09-21. Official ICANN sources only. This note supplies facts for the workflow decision. It does not assess NiceNIC's conduct or any private correspondence.

## Contractual duties

The amendment effective 5 April 2024 restates RAA section 3.18:

- **3.18.1** requires a published abuse email address or web form, receipt confirmation, and reasonable, prompt investigation and an appropriate response to abuse reports.
- **3.18.2** requires prompt, appropriate mitigation once the registrar has actionable evidence of DNS Abuse involving a domain it sponsors. The action must stop or disrupt that use, with attention to harm and collateral damage.
- **3.18.3** requires review within 24 hours for well-founded reports through the dedicated contact for specified authorities in the registrar's jurisdiction. This is not an ordinary reporter's deadline or a takedown deadline.
- **3.18.4** requires published handling procedures and records of receipt and response, retained for two years or the shorter period permitted by law.

DNS Abuse includes phishing and malware, botnets, pharming, and spam that delivers those harms. Ordinary unwanted mail alone does not meet that definition. [2024 RAA amendment](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/accredited-registrars/registrar-accreditation-agreement-global-amendment-05apr24-en.htm)

## Interpretation and limitations

ICANN's advisory is interpretive guidance. Its examples are neither universal deadlines nor guarantees of compliance. Promptness depends on the circumstances and potential harm. A provider's published interval therefore cannot settle whether its actions were contractually prompt.

Actionable evidence is enough information for a reasonable determination of DNS Abuse. An incomplete report still triggers investigation duties. Domain suspension is not always appropriate: a compromised legitimate domain can carry unrelated websites and email. Notification to a host or registrant may be appropriate mitigation. Hosts can address particular content, whereas registrar domain suspension can affect unrelated services. [ICANN DNS Abuse advisory](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/advisories/documents/advisory-compliance-with-dns-abuse-obligations-in-the-registrar-accreditation-agreement-and-the-registry-agreement-05-02-2024-en)

The complaint route is **Abuse/DNS Abuse (Registrar)** on the [Contractual Compliance complaint page](https://www.icann.org/compliance/complaint). ICANN investigates contractual performance. Filing a complaint does not establish a breach, require a particular takedown, or establish a registrar's motive.

Trademark disputes have distinct dispute-resolution routes. A phishing complaint needs evidence of deception and attempted harm, rather than relying solely on a brand name or logo. This distinction follows from the separate [trademark resources](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/trademark-infringement-2017-06-20-en) and the advisory's phishing definition.

## Complaint submission guidance

ICANN's November 2025 guide specifies:

- Report to the registrar or registry first and allow reasonable investigation time. Do not escalate on the same day.
- Identify affected gTLD domains. This route does not enforce ccTLD policies or hosting-provider obligations.
- Explain the abuse and supply evidence. A phishing email is an explicit example; a landing-page screenshot is not the only accepted evidence.
- Include the initial report and subsequent correspondence. Account for requested evidence, including why any request remains unfulfilled.
- Explain the suspected handling failure. Keep allegations consistent between the original report and escalation.
- Supply authorization if claiming to represent an impersonated organization.
- Avoid duplicate complaints. Processing time varies with complexity and completeness.

The guide offers anonymous submission that withholds contact details from the registrar or registry. Permission to share them may later be needed. That option does not promise that identifiers inside attached evidence will be removed. [ICANN submission guide, pages 2–4](https://www.icann.org/en/system/files/files/submitting-dns-abuse-complaints-icann-guide-17nov25-en.pdf)

## Implications for Angry Carp

These are design recommendations derived from the sources, not additional ICANN requirements:

- Keep the routine's follow-up interval separate from contractual expectations. A stalled destination can trigger an escalation assessment without declaring a breach.
- Preserve a case chronology privately: registrar identity, affected domains, exact submitted evidence, submission and receipt dates, ticket references, responses, requested evidence, and remaining gaps.
- Build an escalation dossier across related cases while preserving each case's evidence. Repeated refusals support a description of observed handling, not an assertion that a provider supports criminals.
- If a registrar treats a phishing report as a trademark dispute, compare its explanation with the actual report. Record the mismatch and the specific phishing evidence it did not address.
- Keep complaint drafting subject to approval and manual form submission. Preserve complete originals privately, then review any proposed disclosure and redaction before submission. Scanner privacy rules do not authorize uploading correspondence to ICANN.

No case-specific registrar identity, complaint history, evidence sufficiency, or compliance conclusion was verified in this research.
