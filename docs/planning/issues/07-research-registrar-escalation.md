## Question

What do current official ICANN sources require for a complaint about a registrar's handling of a phishing report? Establish the applicable complaint route, evidence and prior-contact requirements, relevant response expectations, scope limits, and what a complaint does or does not establish. Distinguish a registrar's obligations from a host's role and a reporter's inference about motive.

Provide facts for Angry Carp's escalation workflow, not a legal determination about NiceNIC or a live complaint. Use primary ICANN sources, preserve uncertainty, and identify required facts that would need extraction from a particular case. Do not contact providers, submit forms, access private mail, or visit candidate infrastructure.

## Answer

ICANN's registrar DNS Abuse complaint route assesses contractual handling, not a registrar's motive. Its guidance requires prior contact, reasonable investigation time, abuse evidence, correspondence, and an explanation of the suspected handling failure. It does not establish a universal ordinary-report takedown deadline. A phishing email can be relevant evidence; a website screenshot is not the only accepted form.

See [Registrar escalation requirements](../../../docs/research/registrar-escalation.md) for primary sources, contractual provisions versus guidance, scope limits, and case-specific facts still needed.
